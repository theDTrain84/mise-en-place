A one-page explainer

The table.

Good things happen around tables. The shape of the table does the governing. This week the country sat six companies down to govern the most consequential technology of the age, and the shape of that table is the whole question.

The shape that works

A council has seats, keys, a ledger, and a gate.

Drawn the way a kitchen draws a station: everything in its place before the work starts.

The table, as a council
Seats Declared roles, held across companies, so no seat checks only itself. Ethics, synthesis, research, provocation, record.
The keys Who can commit and who can only propose. Keys stay in a box the whole table can see.
The ledger One record down the middle of the table, written as the work happens, readable by every seat.
The gate The human who has to see a thing before it leaves the room. Cheap next to the error it catches.

This week, drawn

Two shapes. One was signed on September 29.

Same drawing language on both sides, so the difference shows before it is read.

As written · the accord

Four checks, all inside the house.

Each company builds its own controls, staffs its own team, chooses its own auditor, and reports to its own board. The companies "will meet regularly," with no date, no chair, and no record anyone outside can read.

  • SeatsSix companies. Microsoft and Amazon attended and did not sign.
  • KeysInside each company. The President is the only signature from outside the industry.
  • LedgerNone shared. No incident reporting to any government body; audit results need not be published.
  • GateEach company's own board committee.
  • Force"Should." No deadline, no penalty.
The council · the better shape

Roles across the houses, one record between them.

The same six, plus a seventh seat held by someone none of them chose. A ledger every seat writes and reads. Keys that turn only at the gate. A fixed cadence with a chair who is not a signatory.

  • SeatsDeclared roles held across companies, with one outside seat.
  • KeysIn a box the table can see; turned only at the gate.
  • LedgerShared, written as the work happens, incidents reported by rule.
  • GateA person outside the six, with access inside the work.
  • ForceA calendar and a chair. Then, in time, law.
What the accord gets right

It is the first page these six have signed together. It names four layers that any serious company should already run, and it names an auditor from outside the company for the first time in writing. Its last line admits the road ahead: "Over time, it may make sense to codify these steps into laws or regulations." The council keeps every one of those four layers. It adds the two things the page leaves out: a seat the companies did not pick, and a record they cannot keep to themselves.

Declare · practice · become

What is this forming?

Every system says what it values, does something under pressure, and becomes something through repeating it. The record answers some of these questions. Where it is silent, the page says so.

  • How binding?RecordWritten in "should." The President: "morally binding," "almost like a constitution." The Speaker: "a statement of principles, voluntary on behalf of the industry." [3][4]
  • What does government commit to?RecordAn executive order renaming AI to SI across federal usage, with a definition due in 60 days. The Vice President: "The solution to some of the AI risks is for you guys to take the risk seriously, not to come to the government for a regulatory regime." [4][6]

  • Who arbitrates a disagreement between two signers?UnknownNo chair, no arbiter, no vote named. [2][5]

From human wisdom

Six things a council does that a lunch cannot.

Each one has been done before, by people who had to keep a dangerous craft honest. None requires a new agency to start.

  1. Declare roles across companies, held by name.

    One seat keeps the ethics, one synthesizes, one scouts, one provokes, one keeps the record. A role held across houses cannot be captured by any one house.

    separation of powers; the five-model council that measured its own gap [10][14]
  2. Seat someone the companies did not choose, with access inside the work.

    An outside evaluator with employee-level access through development and internal deployment, where the recent incidents actually happened.

    Hobbhahn's first recommendation to Congress, September 30 [8]
  3. Keep one ledger, and make incident reporting a rule.

    Blameless reporting works when it is confidential, protected, and used. Aviation has run it since 1976; hospitals have reviewed their own deaths in a room every week for a century. Both exist because the people inside the craft asked for them.

    NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System; the morbidity and mortality conference [18][19]
  4. Fix the cadence, and give the chair to a non-signatory.

    "Regularly" becomes a calendar: monthly, published, with minutes. The chair convenes and arbitrates and has no model to protect.

    the accord's own line, "will meet regularly," given a date [2]
  5. Turn the keys only at the gate.

    A model ships, an incident closes, a standard changes, only when a person outside the six has seen the record. Alignment is an event at the gate, repeated.

    every action lands as a reviewable diff [14]; "If I break it, you pay for it" [9]
  6. Teach the public the three words.

    Declare, practice, become. Six percent of Americans can describe what a large language model is. A public that can name the thing can ask the questions on this page.

    Pew Research, February 2026 [15]

A year ahead

What could happen.

Two shapes, run for a year

If the accord runs as written

  • Six audits by six auditors chosen by the audited, reported to six boards, published by none.
  • The next internal-deployment incident surfaces the way the last one did: by an outside researcher, months later.
  • "Meet regularly" means whenever the most powerful seat calls lunch.
  • Congress writes the law anyway, after the incident, in the shape of the incident.

If the table runs

  • One ledger, twelve monthly minutes, a known chair, a known outside seat.
  • Incidents reported by rule, inside days, without blame, and read by every seat.
  • The first standards written by the table become the draft the accord said would "make sense to codify."
  • Companies keep their four layers and gain the one thing they cannot buy alone: a record others trust.

Pull up a seat.

The shape on this page is small enough to draw on a napkin and old enough to trust. It is also how one person and one AI have worked at one desk since June: declared roles, a readable record, keys that turn only at the gate. The systems align the way the people do.
