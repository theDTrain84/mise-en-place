The week of September 29, 2026

Six companies signed. Two sat it out. Nobody has to report anything.

On Monday the President and six chief executives signed one page at the White House. It is called the Accord on Super Intelligence. Here is who was at the table, what the page says in its own words, what it binds, what it doesn't, and what would.

Who was at the table

Nine chairs. One at the head. Two left empty on the page.

What they actually signed

Four layers, in their words. What each one means. What each one leaves out.

The page is one sheet and is written in "should." Every quotation below is from the accord's text as published. [2]

1
Robust internal controls to monitor the capabilities and alignment of its models during training and deployment around areas like cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical threats.
What it meansEach company watches its own models for the ways they could be used to attack a network, a lab, or a population.
What it doesn't bindWhat counts as a control, how strong, or by when. No standard is named and no deadline is set.
2
An internal team to ensure all of the controls, monitoring, and detection are operating as intended.
What it meansA safety team inside the company checks the company's own work.
What it doesn't bindThe team reports to the company. Nothing it finds has to leave the building.
3
An independent external auditor to carry out independent assessments of whether the controls, monitoring, and detection are operating as intended.
What it meansFor the first time in writing, these six agree that someone from outside should look.
What it doesn't bindWho the auditor is. Each company picks its own. The audit need not be published, and no result has to be reported to any government body. [3][5]
4
An independent committee of the board of directors to oversee and receive reports and ensure any issues identified are remediated.
What it meansThe company's own board, through a committee, is the last stop for what the team and the auditor find.
What it doesn't bindAnyone outside the company. The board answers to shareholders. No regulator, no legislature, no public reads the report.

The participating companies will meet regularly to establish standards and best practices. No date, no chair, no minutes.

Over time, it may make sense to codify these steps into laws or regulations. The page says the law may come later. It does not say when, or who writes it.

We believe every company is responsible for developing its own technology safely. That sentence is the accord's theory of governance in full: each house keeps its own. [6]

What the page gets right

It is the first sheet these six rivals have signed together. It names the four layers any serious lab should already run, and it writes the words "independent external auditor" into a document with the President's signature on it. Its last line admits the road ahead. Keep all four layers. The rest of this page is about the two things the sheet leaves off: a seat the companies did not pick, and a record they cannot keep to themselves.

The two empty chairs

Microsoft and Amazon came to lunch and left their pens in their pockets.

What the record says

RecordBoth attended the September 29 lunch. Neither signed. [1][4]

UnknownWhy. No statement from either company explaining the decision was found in the record as of October 4. Reporting that Meta's chief executive drafted the page and circulated it is relayed, from one outlet. [4]

RecordBoth companies did sign the previous White House set of voluntary AI commitments, in July 2023, alongside Google, Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic. [19]

What it means for accountability

Nothing in the accord reaches Microsoft or Amazon. Nothing in the accord reaches the six who signed, either. That is the point of the empty chairs: a pledge with no penalty binds the signer and the non-signer the same amount, which is none.

The two companies that sat out run the clouds that most frontier models are trained and served on. A table that governs models and leaves out the floor they stand on has drawn its circle too small.

How it would be governed

Who schedules the meetings?

The record answers some of these. Where it is silent, the page says so.

  • Who schedules the meetings, and how often?Unknown"Will meet regularly." No date, no cadence, no convener in the text. [2]
  • Who arbitrates a disagreement between two signers?UnknownNo chair, no arbiter, no vote named. [2][5]
  • Who audits, and who picks them?RecordAn outside auditor, chosen by the company being audited. [3]
  • What gets reported, to whom?RecordAuditor reports go to the company's own board committee. No incident has to be reported to any government body, and no audit result has to be published. [3][5]
  • What department is this?UnknownThe President said he is considering "a 10-person committee" to oversee the industry and would name a czar within days. As of October 4 no roster and no appointment appear in the record. [4][6]
  • What is the government's own commitment on the page?RecordIts signature. The executive order signed the same day renames the technology ("Super Intelligence," "SI") across federal usage and gives the science adviser 60 days to propose a legal definition. It creates no office, no duty and no oversight body. [6][12]

What right does this have

None in law. And it was signed the same afternoon the Senate was asked to pass a law.

The accord creates no legal duty and no legal right; it is a pledge. The instruments below do bind, or would. Each row says what it reaches that the accord does not. [5]

Is the accord being used as an argument against the bills? In the record found, no official has cited it by name as a reason to stop a bill. The posture is stated plainly instead. The Vice President, Sept. 29: "The solution to some of the AI risks is for you guys to take the risk seriously, not to come to the government for a regulatory regime." The Senate Majority Leader said Congress will need to legislate "at some point." The one bill that reached the floor that day was blocked the same afternoon. [4][15]

What the table shows: the only instruments that bind the companies at the table today are two state laws. Everything federal is a pledge, a vocabulary order, a draft, or a bill waiting on a vote.

The better shape

Now the table.

A council has seats, keys, a ledger and a gate. The reader has seen what the accord is. Here is what it would take to make the same six chairs govern.

The table, as a council
Seats. Declared roles held across companies, so no seat checks only itself.
The keys. Who can commit and who can only propose, in a box the whole table can see.
The ledger. One record down the middle, written as the work happens, readable by every seat.
The gate. The person outside the six who has to see a thing before it leaves the room.
Six things a council does that a lunch cannot
  1. Declare roles across companies, held by name.

    One seat keeps the ethics, one synthesizes, one scouts, one provokes, one keeps the record. A role held across houses cannot be captured by one house.

    separation of powers
  2. Seat someone the companies did not choose, with access inside the work.

    Employee-level access through development and internal deployment, where the recent incidents actually happened.

    Hobbhahn's first recommendation to the Senate, Sept. 30 [8]; the 45-day access in the bill blocked Sept. 29 [15]
  3. Keep one ledger, and make incident reporting a rule.

    Blameless, confidential, protected, and used. Aviation has run it since 1976; hospitals have reviewed their own deaths in a room every week for a century. California already requires it in 15 days; New York in 72 hours.

    NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System; the morbidity and mortality conference; SB 53; RAISE [22][23][13][14]
  4. Fix the cadence, and give the chair to a non-signatory.

    "Regularly" becomes a calendar: monthly, published, with minutes. The chair convenes and arbitrates and has no model to protect.

    the accord's own line, given a date [2]
  5. Turn the keys only at the gate.

    A model ships, an incident closes, a standard changes, only when a person outside the six has seen the record.

    "If I break it, you pay for it," the chair of the Sept. 30 hearing [9]; the Oct. 1 liability bill [18]
  6. Teach the public the three words.

    Declare, practice, become. Six percent of Americans can describe what a large language model is. A public that can name the thing can ask the questions on this page.

    Pew Research, February 2026 [11]

If the accord runs a year as written: six audits by six auditors chosen by the audited, reported to six boards, published by none, and the next internal incident surfaces the way the last one did, by an outside researcher, months later. If the table runs a year: one ledger, twelve sets of minutes, a known chair, a known outside seat, and the first standards the table writes become the draft the accord said would "make sense to codify."

Pull up a seat.

The shape on this page is small enough to draw on a napkin and old enough to trust. It is also how one person and one AI have worked at one desk since June: declared roles, a readable record, keys that turn only at the gate. The systems align the way the people do.
